# amth491-cl

This undergraduate senior thesis project seeks to investigate whether the Ratings Percentage Index (RPI) systematically over-values teams from stronger Division 1 conferences as a metric of comparison for the NCAA Division 1 Men’s College Soccer Championship. The project will begin with a formal mathematical analysis of the RPI formula as used by NCAA Men’s Soccer and the intuition behind its potential bias towards teams from stronger Division 1 conferences in contrast to a more standard Bradley-Terry pairwise comparison model. Then, NCAA regular-season games will be simulated to create synthetic RPI and Bradley-Terry rankings and to compare the relative under-performance by teams from stronger conferences across the two pairwise comparison models. After that, the inference methods will be applied to real-life NCAA college soccer data to reveal how simulation patterns hold with real-life results. Lastly, a detailed analysis of findings will provide insights on the value of RPI as a metric for evaluating NCAA men’s soccer teams across conferences in the NCAA tournament selection process.
